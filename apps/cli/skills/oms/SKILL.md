---
name: oms
description: Make, inspect, revise or render OpenMotionStudio (OMS) motion videos from a brief and local assets, end to end to an MP4. Use for requests to make a video with OMS, to render or update an existing film.tsx, or to create a project to refine in Studio, in any language.
---

Making a film is a creative task. It is finished when someone who has not read the script would be glad to publish it, not when `oms check` is clean. Each scene needs a visual idea of its own subject (a metaphor, a drawing driven by its data, one object that transforms, a move of the frame) and motion that carries meaning.

Its commands come from the `oms` package: when `oms` is not found, install it with `npm install -g @motionhub/oms` (Node 22.12 or newer).

Read the bundled [film format](film-format.md) first, then the [example film](example/film.tsx) (copy its structure, not its look). Read each reference below when its step comes.

## Order of work

1. **Start or read.** For an existing project, read film.tsx, its parts, media and motion rules, and keep edits and identifiers outside the request; reread the on-disk source on every later turn. For a new one run `oms init "<new folder>"`.
2. **Facts, then script.** Gather what the film says; never invent a figure. Write the lines for 75 to 90 percent of the length asked, one idea a line ([craft](references/craft.md), "Script").
3. **Board each scene before drawing it.** Its lines; its base picture as layers with boxes in px; for each line, what changes and when. Think of at least two ideas that differ in kind and take the one a newcomer would understand.
4. **Settle the look.** By default the film has a look of its own: write it in `parts/kit.tsx` from the subject's own world ([look](references/look.md)), its faces taken with `oms font` (a Chinese film needs one for its script; import its files by the paths it prints). Use a motion only when the project already has one or the person asks for one by name or by look ("in ink-scroll", "use a motion"): then find it with `oms motion search <words>` (motions with any of the words, those with more first; it lists the words motions are tagged with when none matches) and take it with `oms motion use <@scope/slug> "<folder>"` (downloaded then, once). Do not search motionhub otherwise.
5. **Draw one scene, look at it, then the rest.** Write no second scene before the first has passed `oms check` and you have looked at a still of it: what is wrong with one scene (sizes, the layout, the faces, the caption) is then fixed once, not in every scene. film.tsx holds the scenes, layers and lines; each scene's picture is its own `parts/<scene>.tsx` ([drawing](references/drawing.md)). After the first scene is drawn and looked at, draw the others simply, so a whole plain film exists within the first third of your time, then improve the weakest scenes. A complete plain film is a video; a half-polished one is not.
6. **Sound, when the film wants it.** Music the person gives goes into `media/` with its licence noted ([assets](references/assets.md)). Effects come from `oms sound`, in the motion's own sound when the film draws in one and in oms's own otherwise: list the moments the picture makes as cues, in the roles the sound has (of `land`, `move`, `draw`, `count`, `cut`, `out`; `oms sound` names them and refuses any other) and run `oms sound "<folder>" --cues "1.2:land, 3.4:move:0.6" --seconds <film.seconds from `oms check --json`> --out "<folder>/media/effects.wav"` (`--out` is a path from where you run it, as for `still` and `render`), then play it across the film with `<Sound src="media/effects.wav" />`, as it comes mixed, 3 to 6 dB lower under music. Read the levels it prints: a cue marked not heard needs another role ([craft](references/craft.md), "Sound"). A new `--out` each time the cues change. **A narration, when the person asks for one**: give `<Film>` its voice (`voice="minimax:male-qn-jingying"`, for Chinese a MiniMax voice, for English an ElevenLabs one; take the name from `oms voice --list`, which is what the account offers), finish the words first (each spoken line is paid for), run `oms voice "<folder>"`, then `oms check`: each voiced line now lasts as long as its speech, so fit the scenes and the picture to it, and use `<Film captions="words">` to light each word as it is said. A person who reads it themselves records one take of the lines, as written, and `oms voice "<folder>" --recording <file>` cuts it into the lines, in `<Film voice="recording:take">`: what to ask them for, and how to read what it prints (a line read otherwise is cut all the same, its caption showing the written words), are in [craft](references/craft.md), "Sound".
7. **Review in rounds, at most three.** A round is `oms check "<folder>" --json`, stills where you doubt, and one pass of fixes ([review](references/review.md)). Fix everything a viewer would notice in a scene in one edit.
8. **Render and report.** `oms render "<folder>" --out "<new path>.mp4"` needs the person's ffmpeg; if it says ffmpeg is missing or incomplete, pass on its install command. It also writes the lines as an `.srt` beside the MP4, and as a subtitle track in it only when the film does not draw them as captions. Return the MP4, the project folder and the duration. When the person wants the film on the web, `oms player "<folder>" --out "<new folder>"` writes a folder any static host plays without a render, or its index.html opened from disk (`oms preview "<folder>"` plays it on this machine and follows changes). The folder also opens in Studio, with its Import project action, for refining by hand; to send it to the Studio on another machine, or to a person, `oms pack "<folder>"` writes it as one film file beside it (`<folder>.oms`, without renders, stills or the Studio's state), which the Studio's Import project opens as a new project.

Assets the person gives or you fetch follow [assets](references/assets.md).

## In this host

The first `oms check`, `still`, `render` or `voice` downloads the Chrome it draws in once (about 100 MB, unless HyperFrames or Puppeteer already has it), and each may say Chrome runs without its sandbox where it cannot start one: neither is an error. The film format names the Studio's tools: here `check` is `oms check`, `still` is `oms still "<folder>" --at <seconds> --out "<new path>.png"` (a time strictly before the film's end; a new path each time), `search_motions` is `oms motion search`, `use_motion` is `oms motion use`, `use_font` is `oms font "<folder>" --family "<family>" --weights 400,700`, `list_voices` is `oms voice --list <minimax|elevenlabs|openai|azure>` and `voice_lines` is `oms voice "<folder>"`. A voice provider is set up not in Models but by its key in the environment oms runs in (`MINIMAX_API_KEY`, with `MINIMAX_REGION=china` or `MINIMAX_BASE_URL=https://api.minimaxi.com` for an account on minimaxi.com; `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`; `OPENAI_API_KEY`; `AZURE_SPEECH_KEY` with `AZURE_SPEECH_REGION`): if the one the film needs is missing, `oms voice` says which variable, and you ask the person to set it; never ask for a key in the conversation or write one anywhere.
